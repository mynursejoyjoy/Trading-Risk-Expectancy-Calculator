2. Kelly Criterion

Core Principle: Proposed by John L. Kelly Jr., the Kelly Criterion calculates the optimal fraction of capital to risk per trade given a known Win Rate (W) and Risk-Reward Ratio (R) to maximize long-term compound growth while avoiding ruin.

Kelly % = Win Rate - (Loss Rate / Risk-Reward Ratio)

How to Use: If Kelly % is 0% or negative, the setup lacks positive expectancy and should not be traded. Because Full Kelly creates large equity swings, most traders use Half-Kelly (Kelly ÷ 2) or Quarter-Kelly (Kelly ÷ 4) as a practical risk cap.

3. Monte Carlo Simulation

Core Principle: Even with positive expectancy, the order of wins and losses in real trading is random (e.g., 5 losses in a row can happen anytime). Monte Carlo simulation runs randomized trade sequences to show realistic equity paths and drawdown risk.

How to Use: This calculator simulates multiple random sequences using your Win Rate and R:R, then plots the median path against the dashed blue Theoretical Expectancy Curve. Click "Re-run Simulation" several times to inspect Max Drawdown and Max Losing Streak to ensure your risk % fits your tolerance.
